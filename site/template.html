What a housing association actually does

A shared map of social housing in two views. Services describe what residents, communities and colleagues are trying to get done, in their own words. The operating model describes how the organisation does the work behind them, down to individual activities. Each service is linked to the activities it relies on.

About this model

The structure is based on what every English provider has to do: the Regulator of Social Housing's consumer and economic standards, landlord safety duties (gas, electrical, fire, asbestos, water and lifts), the Building Safety Act, HHSRS, Awaab's Law, the Housing Ombudsman's Complaint Handling Code and the Tenant Satisfaction Measures, plus the way the sector usually organises its teams and costs.

I was originally inspired to create this after reading Rannoch Associates' paper "The impact of AI on Housing Associations" (October 2026). That model wasn't published, but it did make me think that a common and open model for services could be useful for others in the sector, so this is an independent attempt, not their work. I was also inspired by Dave Skinner's work around Housing Service Patterns: the service names and interaction patterns are adapted from the social housing service list by Design Patterns for Social Housing, whose pattern groups were mapped from TPXImpact's work on local government services. Several requests were added after a review of Anchor Housing's customer journey catalogue, with thanks. Every name and grouping is a judgement about a generic association. Real organisations differ, and that's what makes it worth improving together.
